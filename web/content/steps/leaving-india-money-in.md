#### What it is

Bringing money to India and sending it back later are different things with different rules.

#### When

Before you move large amounts.
Ask your Indian bank what proof it needs.

#### Forms and fees

- Bringing money in: RBI's FAQ says there is no limit on foreign exchange you bring in. Declare to Customs if notes, bank notes and travellers cheques together exceed USD 10,000, or foreign currency notes alone exceed USD 5,000. That FAQ is written for travellers returning from a trip, so check with your bank if you are relocating.
- Foreign exchange balances can go into an RFC account.
- Sending money out of India later: Form 145 (formerly Form 15CA) is the information form for a payment to a non-resident. Part A is for payments up to Rs 5 lakh in the tax year. Above that you need an order from the Assessing Officer (Part B) or an accountant's certificate in Form 146 (Part C, formerly Form 15CB).
- Forms 145 and 146 are for money going out. They are not for money coming in.
- NRIs can send up to USD 1 million per financial year from NRO balances and sale proceeds, with documents and taxes paid. That route is for people who are still non-resident.

#### What to watch for

- No official page we read says what tax applies to the transfer itself. Ask your bank and a CA.
- Which limit applies to money you send out after you become resident is not stated on RBI's NRI page. For residents, the Liberalised Remittance Scheme limit is USD 250,000 per financial year.
- The Income-tax Rules, 2026 took effect on April 1, 2026 and were amended after. Check the current rule before you send money.
- Talk to a CA before you send large sums either way.
