#### What it is

You have four choices for your 401(k) when you leave a job.

- Leave it where it is.
- Roll it into a new employer's plan.
- Roll it into a traditional or Roth IRA.
- Cash it out.

A direct rollover moves the money straight from the plan to the new plan or IRA. No tax is withheld and none is due.
A cash-out makes the untaxed part income. The 10% additional tax also applies if you are under 59½. There is no exception to it for moving abroad.

#### When

Decide before your last day.
Tell the plan your new address as soon as you have one, and ask what it will withhold.

#### Forms and fees

- A plan paying a foreign person must generally withhold 30%. The 20% rate you see on US sites is for US persons.
- The plan reports a payment to a foreign person on Form 1042-S, not Form 1099-R.
- If a payment is made to you, you have 60 days to roll it over. To roll over the full amount, you must replace the withheld part from other money.
- India's 0% treaty rate covers pensions and annuities paid as periodic payments. It does not cover a lump sum.
- If your balance is $7,000 or less, the plan may pay it out without asking you. Between $1,000 and $7,000 it must move the money to an IRA if you make no choice.
- Form 5329 reports the 10% additional tax. It can go with your Form 1040-NR.

#### What to watch for

- The plan decides from your forms whether you are a US person. With no W-9 or W-8BEN, it presumes you are a US person only if it has both your SSN and a US address.
- The 30% is withholding. It is not always your final tax. A cash-out can be split into a part taxed at the normal rates and a part taxed at a flat 30%.
- An unpaid 401(k) loan counts as a payment when you leave. You can roll over an amount equal to it by the due date of your return, with extensions.
- A Roth 401(k) payout that does not qualify is split between contributions and earnings, so part of it is taxable.
- Talk to a CPA before you cash out. The tax depends on how the plan classifies you, where you live and how the treaty applies.
