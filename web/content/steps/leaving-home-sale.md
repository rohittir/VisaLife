#### What it is

If you sell your main home, you can leave out up to $250,000 of gain. The limit is $500,000 for a married couple filing jointly.
You must have owned it and lived in it as your main home for at least 24 months in the 5 years before the sale.
A non-resident alien can use this too. Because a non-resident alien who files Form 1040-NR cannot file jointly, the limit is $250,000.

#### When

Before you set a closing date.
Ask a CPA how your residency end date affects the sale.

#### Forms and fees

- If you do not meet the 24-month tests, you can leave out part of the gain when the main reason for the sale is a work move, a health move or an unforeseeable event.
- If you are a foreign person when you sell, the buyer must withhold 15% of the price. This is the price, not your gain.
- If the buyer will live in the home, there is no withholding at $300,000 or less. Above that, it is 10% up to $1,000,000 and 15% over that.
- Form 8288-B asks the IRS to reduce or remove the withholding. The IRS generally answers within about 90 days.
- The buyer files Forms 8288 and 8288-A. You attach the stamped Form 8288-A to your return to claim the tax withheld.

#### What to watch for

- FIRPTA withholding applies to foreign persons. A resident alien gives the buyer a certification of non-foreign status instead, for example a Form W-9.
- No IRS page we read says how to decide your status on a closing date in the year you leave. It depends on your residency end date.
- Talk to a CPA before you sign a sale contract in the year you leave.
