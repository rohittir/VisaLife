#### What it is

H-1B portability lets you start work for a new employer as soon as USCIS receives the new employer's petition.
You do not wait for approval.
You qualify if all of these are true:

- you were lawfully admitted in H-1B status,
- the new employer filed a non-frivolous H-1B petition before your authorized stay ended, and
- you have not worked without authorization since your last admission.

#### When

You can start on the day USCIS receives the petition, or on the start date it requests, whichever is later.
A petition that USCIS rejects has no filing date, so it gives you nothing to start on.

#### Forms and fees

Your new employer files Form I-129 with a certified Labor Condition Application.
Premium processing costs $2,965 and gets a decision in 15 business days. It does not change the day you can start.

#### What to watch for

- If USCIS denies the petition, your work for the new employer stops. You may go back to your old employer if that approval is still valid.
- A new employer needs a cap registration only if you were never counted against the H-1B cap in the last six years. This mostly affects people moving from a university or nonprofit research employer.
- You can work for more than one H-1B employer at once. Each employer files its own petition.
- No USCIS page sets a maximum gap between jobs. During a gap you rely on the 60-day grace period.
- With an approved I-140, check the 180-day rule before the old employer withdraws it.
