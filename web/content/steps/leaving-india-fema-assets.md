#### What it is

FEMA is the law that controls foreign exchange in India. It has a rule for people who come home with assets abroad.
Section 6(4) lets a resident hold, own, transfer or invest in foreign currency, foreign securities and foreign property. The assets must have been acquired, held or owned while you lived outside India, or inherited from someone who did.

#### When

Before you return.
Write down what you own abroad and the date you acquired each asset. The date matters.

#### Forms and fees

- You can keep a foreign currency account you opened while you were non-resident.
- You can use those assets, their income and their sale proceeds for payments and for new overseas investments without RBI approval. The cost must come only from those assets, and the transaction must be otherwise allowed.
- New foreign assets bought after you become resident go through the Liberalised Remittance Scheme. The limit is USD 250,000 per financial year.

#### What to watch for

- The RBI circular that explains section 6(4) is from January 2014. We did not check whether the Overseas Investment Rules, 2022 restate or replace it.
- Keeping an asset under FEMA is separate from reporting it for tax. See the step on Schedule FA.
- Talk to a CA or CPA before you buy new foreign assets after you return.
