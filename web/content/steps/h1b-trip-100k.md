#### What it is

A September 2025 proclamation requires a $100,000 payment with some new H-1B petitions. Your employer pays it, through pay.gov, before filing.
It covers petitions filed on or after Sept. 21, 2025 for people outside the US who do not hold a valid H-1B visa.
It also covers a change-of-status or extension petition if USCIS finds the person is not eligible for it, for example because they left before a decision.

#### When

It matters when a new petition is filed while you are abroad.
It does not apply to current H-1B visa holders who travel in and out, or to granted extensions, amendments and changes of status for people inside the US.

#### Forms and fees

The payment is $100,000, once per new petition. Exceptions are described as "extraordinarily rare".
You do not pay it yourself.

#### What to watch for

- On June 8, 2026 a federal court in Massachusetts vacated USCIS's guidance for implementing the payment. The First Circuit denied a stay on July 24, 2026.
- USCIS says it will comply with the order while DHS considers next steps, and that it still plans to collect the payment if the order is lifted.
- On Sept. 18, 2026 the President extended the proclamation to Sept. 21, 2027. Other lawsuits are still pending.
- Leaving the US before USCIS decides a status change can bring the payment into play. Ask your employer or counsel before you go.
