#### What it is

Some EB-2 workers have their employer file a new EB-3 I-140 and keep the earlier priority date.
This only helps when the EB-3 date for India is ahead of the EB-2 date.
If your I-485 is already pending, you can ask USCIS to move it to the new category. USCIS calls this a transfer of underlying basis. People also call it interfiling.

#### When

Only when the bulletin shows the new category ahead of yours.
For a transfer, a visa must be immediately available in the new category on the day you file the request.

#### Forms and fees

- Your employer files a new Form I-140 in EB-3. You do not file a new I-485 or pay a new I-485 fee.
- You send USCIS a written request to transfer the pending I-485 to the new petition.
- A transfer to an I-140 that is already approved needs Form I-485 Supplement J.

#### What to watch for

- You keep your earlier priority date only if your first I-140 is approved and not revoked (8 CFR 204.5(e)).
- USCIS says not to assume a transfer will be granted. It does not tell you when it is.
- After a transfer, the first I-140 no longer supports the I-485. You can transfer back with a new request.
- The 180-day portability count starts again, and a child's CSPA age is recalculated on the new petition.
- Whether your PERM can support the EB-3 I-140 depends on its job requirements. This is a strategic decision. Talk to an immigration attorney.
