#### What it is

In the year you leave, you are a US tax resident until your residency end date and a non-resident after it. This is called a dual-status year.
You are a resident for a year if you were in the US at least 31 days and your weighted days reach 183.
Weighted days are all your days this year, plus a third of last year's and a sixth of the year before.
H-1B days count. F-1 student days do not count until you have been exempt for part of more than 5 calendar years, unless you show you do not plan to stay.

#### When

The return is due in the spring after you leave. For 2026 that is April 15, 2027 if you had wages with tax withheld, or June 15, 2027 if you had none.
An extension gives six more months to file, not to pay.
Your residency end date is December 31 unless an earlier date qualifies.
An earlier date needs a tax home abroad and a closer connection to another country for the rest of the year. It is lost if you are a US resident at any time the next year.

#### Forms and fees

- File Form 1040-NR marked as a dual-status return. Attach a Form 1040 for the resident part, marked "Dual-Status Statement".
- For an earlier end date, attach a signed statement with the facts. It is due with the return.
- F-1, J, M and Q students file Form 8843 to leave out their student days. Attach it to your return. With no return, mail it alone to the IRS in Austin, Texas by the same due date.
- Form 8822 tells the IRS your new address. It takes a foreign address.
- The 2025 edition of Pub 519 says a dual-status return cannot be e-filed. Check the 2026 instructions when they come out.

#### What to watch for

- A dual-status return has no standard deduction, no head of household status and no joint return. If you are married, you use the married filing separately column.
- An F-1 student with no taxable US income does not need a Form 1040-NR, but still files Form 8843.
- File Form 8843 on time. One IRS page says a late form can cost you the exclusion for those days.
- If your accounts abroad together went over $10,000 at any time and you were a resident alien for part of the year, you may need to file an FBAR. How the $10,000 is counted in the year you leave is a CPA question.
- Talk to a CPA before you pick your residency end date or sell anything in the year you leave.
