#### What it is

Once you are resident and ordinarily resident, India expects you to report foreign assets and foreign income in your return.

- Schedule FA lists foreign assets: bank accounts, shares and debt, insurance, property, signing authority and trusts.
- Schedule FSI lists income from outside India. Schedule TR shows the tax relief you claim.
- RNORs and non-residents do not fill Schedule FA or FSI.

#### When

In the return for each year you are ROR.
On the forms in use, Schedule FA covers assets you held at any time in the calendar year ending December 31. We could not confirm whether returns for tax year 2026-27 keep that basis.

#### Forms and fees

- Use ITR-2 or ITR-3. ITR-1 and ITR-4 cannot be used if you have assets outside India, signing authority over a foreign account, or income from outside India.
- Values are converted at the SBI telegraphic transfer buying rate.

#### What to watch for

- The Black Money Act, section 43, lets the Assessing Officer direct a penalty of Rs 10 lakh if you file a return but do not disclose a foreign asset or foreign income. It applies to a resident other than NOR.
- Since October 1, 2024, that section does not apply to assets other than immovable property with a total value up to Rs 20 lakh. Foreign immovable property has no such limit.
- The Finance Act 2026 added the same Rs 20 lakh carve-out to the prosecution sections, from October 1, 2024.
- The carve-out speaks of assets. It does not clearly cover foreign income. Ask a CA.
- We could not confirm how the residence test in the Black Money Act reads from tax year 2026-27. Keep your reporting careful from your first resident year.
- Talk to a CA before you file your first return as a resident.
