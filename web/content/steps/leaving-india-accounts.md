#### What it is

When you return to India, your NRI bank accounts change. The rules are in RBI's Master Direction on Deposits and Accounts.

- NRE account: designate it as a resident account, or move the balance to an RFC account, immediately on your return.
- NRO account: you may redesignate it as a resident rupee account on your return.
- FCNR(B) deposit: it may run to maturity at the contracted rate. Then you convert it to a resident deposit, or to an RFC account if you qualify.
- RFC (Resident Foreign Currency) account: a resident can hold foreign currency in it. It can take NRE and FCNR(B) balances when you become resident. It can also take pension, superannuation and other benefits paid by an overseas employer.

#### When

Plan it before you land.
RBI's word for the NRE account is "immediately". We found no grace period in the Master Direction or in RBI's FAQs.

#### Forms and fees

Your bank handles the change. Each bank sets its own forms, so ask yours which ones it needs.

#### What to watch for

- RFC eligibility is a list of categories. A plain US savings balance is not named. The closest category is assets you acquired while non-resident and brought to India. Ask your bank how it will treat your balance.
- Money in an RFC account is free to use outside India.
- Talk to a CA or CPA before you move large balances. Your bank decides how it handles each one.
