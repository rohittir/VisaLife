#### What it is

India taxes you by your residential status for each tax year. A tax year runs from April 1 to March 31.
There are three statuses: non-resident, resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR), and resident and ordinarily resident (ROR).

You are a resident for a tax year if either test is met.

- You were in India 182 days or more in that tax year.
- You were in India 60 days or more in that tax year and 365 days or more in the 4 years before it.

You are RNOR if you are a resident and either of these holds.

- You were a non-resident in 9 of the 10 tax years before.
- You were in India 729 days or less in the 7 tax years before.

Other RNOR cases apply to citizens and people of Indian origin with high Indian income who visit.
The rules are in section 6 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

#### When

The 2025 Act applies from tax year 2026-27, which began April 1, 2026. Earlier years stay under the 1961 Act.
Days in earlier years still count in the look-backs.
For someone who was abroad about ten years and stays in India, RNOR typically lasts the year you return and one or two more. That is arithmetic from the two tests, not an official figure. It depends on your days.

#### Forms and fees

Section 5 of the Act sets what is taxed.

- Non-resident: income received or arising in India.
- RNOR: income received or arising in India. Foreign income is taxed only if it comes from a business controlled in India or a profession set up in India.
- ROR: income from everywhere in the world.

#### What to watch for

- A long stay in your first two years uses up the 729-day test. Count your days.
- The pages we read do not say whether remitting earlier-earned US income into India counts as "received in India". Ask a CPA.
- A citizen or person of Indian origin "on a visit" has special rules. The pages we read do not say whether someone moving back for good counts as a visitor. Ask a CPA about your arrival year.
- RNOR status does not change US tax. The US still taxes your US income under its own rules.
- Talk to a CPA with India experience. These first years decide a lot.
