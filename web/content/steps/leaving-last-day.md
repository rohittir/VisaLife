#### What it is

Your status does not end the day your job or program does. A grace period gives you time to leave, change status or find another job.
You cannot work during it unless you are separately authorized.

- H-1B: up to 60 consecutive days after the job ends, or until your authorized stay ends if that comes first. You get it once per validity period.
- H-4: your family follows the H-1B holder. The 60 days cover dependants, and there is no separate clock.
- F-1: 60 days after your program ends, or after your OPT or STEM OPT ends. If your DSO approved a withdrawal, it is 15 days.
- F-1 on OPT: the clock starts on your OPT end date, not on your last day at work.

#### When

Write down your last day of work and your last paid day. Ask your employer to confirm both in writing.
The regulation does not say which day counts as day one. An archived USCIS page says it starts the day after your employment ends, usually your last paid day.
Sixty days is the most you get, not the amount you must use. Your grace period ends when you leave the US.

#### Forms and fees

None for the grace period itself.
Before it ends you can leave, change status, or have a new employer file a petition for you.

#### What to watch for

- DHS proposed on Sept. 11, 2026 to remove the H-1B 60-day grace period. It is only a proposal, comments close Nov. 10, 2026, and today's rule still applies.
- A final rule that would cut the F-1 grace period to 30 days is on hold by court order as of this review.
- You cannot re-enter in F-1 status during the F-1 grace period. To come back and study, you need a new I-20.
- If your employer dismisses you before your authorized stay ends, it owes the reasonable cost of your return trip abroad. Leaving by choice is not a dismissal.
- Your F-2 family's stay depends on your F-1 status. Ask your DSO for their dates.
- Choosing what to do in these days is a strategy question. Talk to an immigration attorney.
