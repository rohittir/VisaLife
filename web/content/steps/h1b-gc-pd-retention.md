#### What it is

Once an I-140 is approved, its priority date is yours for later employment-based petitions, even with a new employer.
If you have several approved petitions, the earliest date wins.
You lose the date only if the approval is revoked for fraud or willful misrepresentation, DOL revokes the PERM, the PERM is invalidated, or USCIS finds a material error.

#### When

Count 180 days from the approval date on your I-140 approval notice.

- If the employer withdraws the I-140 less than 180 days after approval, the approval is automatically revoked, unless your I-485 has been pending 180 days or more.
- If it is withdrawn 180 days or more after approval, the I-140 "remains approved" unless revoked on other grounds.

#### Forms and fees

A new employer's I-140 should include a statement asking for your earlier priority date, with a copy of the old I-797.
The new employer still needs its own PERM.

#### What to watch for

- An I-140 that stays approved after 180 days still supports H-1B extensions beyond six years and job portability.
- Withdrawal is not one of the four ways to lose the date. A withdrawal under 180 days does cancel the approval that your extensions and portability rely on.
- Ask for your own copy of the I-140 approval notice. Do not leave a job without checking the approval date.
