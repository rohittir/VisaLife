#### What it is

When an H-1B job ends, you and your H-4 family are not treated as out of status for up to 60 consecutive days.
The period ends sooner if your authorized validity period ends first.
You get it once per authorized validity period. You cannot work during it unless you are separately authorized.

#### When

The regulation does not say which day counts as day one.
An archived USCIS page says it starts the day after employment ends, usually the last day you were paid.
Write down your last day of work and your last paid day, and ask your employer to confirm both in writing.

#### Forms and fees

Within the grace period you can:

- have a new employer file an H-1B petition for you, and start when USCIS receives it,
- change status with Form I-539, which costs $470 on paper and $420 online,
- file an I-485, if a visa is available to you,
- apply for a compelling-circumstances EAD (Form I-765, category (c)(35)), if you have an approved I-140 and your priority date is not current,
- leave the US.

#### What to watch for

- DHS proposed on Sept. 11, 2026 to remove the grace period. It is only a proposal, comments close Nov. 10, 2026, and today's rule still applies.
- A timely, non-frivolous change-of-status filing stops unlawful presence while it is pending. It does not let you work.
- Job loss alone is generally not enough for a compelling-circumstances EAD. It is granted for one year at a time.
- An employer that dismisses you early owes the reasonable cost of your return trip abroad. Leaving by choice is not a dismissal.
- Choosing between these options is a strategic decision. Talk to an immigration attorney.
