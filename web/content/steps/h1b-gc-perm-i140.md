#### What it is

An employer-sponsored green card has three steps, in this order.

- PERM: your employer gets a prevailing wage determination (Form ETA-9141), recruits for the job, and files Form ETA-9089 with the Department of Labor.
- I-140: your employer files this petition with USCIS, with the certified PERM.
- I-485: you file this once a visa is available for your priority date. Each family member files their own.

Your priority date is the date DOL accepted your PERM for processing.

#### When

DOL certification is valid for 180 days. Your employer must file the I-140 within that time.
DOL posts its current PERM processing times on its FLAG site.

#### Forms and fees

- Your employer must pay PERM costs, including its attorney's fees. It may not charge them back to you. You may pay for your own lawyer.
- I-140: $715 on paper, plus an asylum program fee. The employer pays.
- Premium processing for the I-140 costs $2,965 and takes 15 business days for EB-2 and EB-3. It does not speed PERM.

#### What to watch for

- Keep your own copy of the PERM filing date and the I-140 approval notice. Add your priority date in Me.
- The I-140 is not properly filed without the certified PERM.
- Your six-year H-1B limit can be extended once 365 days have passed since the PERM was filed. See the step on extending past six years.
- Check that the I-140 approval notice shows the category and priority date you expect.
