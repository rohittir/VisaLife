#### What it is

Leaving the US can end a case that is still pending. What happens depends on the case.

- I-485 (green card): leaving without advance parole generally abandons it.
- I-539 to change status: USCIS generally treats it as abandoned if you leave before a decision.
- H-1B change of status, for example F-1 to H-1B: leaving abandons the change. An approval then comes as a notice for a consulate, not as H-1B status.
- H-1B extension: leaving after you file is generally not a reason to deny it.

#### When

Before you book your flight.
Ask the attorney who filed the case what leaving will do to it.

#### Forms and fees

- The I-485 exception for H-1B: an applicant is not treated as abandoning if they come back still eligible for H-1B, to resume work with the same employer, with a valid H-1B visa if one is needed.
- The exception for H-4: the H-1B holder must still keep H-1B status, the H-4 must still be available, and the H-4 visa must be valid if one is needed.
- Advance parole is Form I-131. Wait for the approved document before you rely on it. It does not guarantee you are let back in. CBP decides.

#### What to watch for

- The exception is written for a trip that ends with a return to the same job. It is not written for someone leaving for good.
- No USCIS page we read says what leaving does to a pending I-539 extension of stay for an H-4. Ask counsel.
- USCIS says the $100,000 payment can apply to a change-of-status petition if the person leaves before it is decided. Check the USCIS H-1B page.
- Talk to an immigration attorney before you leave with any case pending.
