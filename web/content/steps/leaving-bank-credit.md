#### What it is

Your US bank accounts and cards do not close on their own when you leave.
A bank or card issuer can close an account on its own terms. You decide what to keep open, so decide before you go.

#### When

Several weeks before you leave.
Start with the accounts you still need: pay, bills, loan payments and anything that receives refunds.

#### Forms and fees

- Tell each bank and card issuer your new address.
- Pull your free credit reports. The three bureaus give a free report every week at AnnualCreditReport.com.
- A credit freeze is free to place and lift. It stops new accounts being opened in your name. You place it with each of the three bureaus.
- US bank deposits are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. You do not need to be a citizen or resident.

#### What to watch for

- The CFPB says a bank can close a checking account without your permission, and some states require notice. We found no official page on how banks treat customers who move abroad. Ask each bank what it needs from you.
- No official page we read says whether you can keep a US card or account from abroad. Ask the issuer.
- Accounts you hold in India can bring a US reporting rule. See the step on the tax year you leave.
- Talk to a lawyer if a bank closes an account and holds your money.
