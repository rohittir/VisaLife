#### What it is

If the terms of your job change in a material way, your employer must file an amended or new H-1B petition.
Moving to a work location that needs a new LCA is a material change.
A move outside the area of intended employment on your LCA is the usual example.

#### When

Before the change.
A rule effective Jan. 17, 2025 wrote this into 8 CFR 214.2(h)(2)(i)(E). It follows USCIS's 2015 Simeio guidance.

#### Forms and fees

Your employer files an amended Form I-129 with a new LCA.
It pays the fees.

#### What to watch for

- You do not need an amendment for a new location inside the same area of intended employment on your LCA.
- You do not need one for a short-term placement elsewhere of up to 30 days in a year, or up to 60 days if you keep your office at the permanent site and live in its area.
- Non-worksite training and peripatetic duties are also outside the rule, within its time limits.
- USCIS says you can work under the new terms once the employer properly files a non-frivolous petition. Confirm with your employer's lawyer first.
- Moving house to another city, even to work remotely, can change the area of employment.
