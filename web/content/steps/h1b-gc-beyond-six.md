#### What it is

H-1B time normally ends at six years. Two rules let you go beyond it while you wait for a green card.

- One-year extensions (AC21 section 106(a)): at least 365 days have passed since your PERM was filed, or since your I-140 was filed if no PERM was needed.
- Three-year extensions (AC21 section 104(c)): you have an approved I-140, and no visa is available because of the per-country limit.

#### When

For the one-year rule, the 365 days run from filing, not approval.
Your employer can file the H-1B extension up to six months before the start date, even before day 365, if the PERM or I-140 will be 365 days old when the extension starts.
For the three-year rule, the I-140 must be approved and the visa unavailable on the day the H-1B petition is filed.

#### Forms and fees

Your employer files a new Form I-129 with a certified LCA and marks the AC21 reason.
Premium processing costs $2,965 for a decision in 15 business days.

#### What to watch for

- Extensions continue until a final decision on your PERM, I-140 or I-485. You can keep extending while the I-485 is pending.
- Only the main beneficiary of the PERM or I-140 qualifies. Your H-1B employer need not be the one that filed it.
- You lose the one-year rule if you hold an approved I-140 and do not file your I-485 within a year after a visa is authorized for issuance, unless something outside your control stopped you.
- A pending I-485 gives you no status. Keep your H-1B valid.
- Count days spent outside the US first. They may add time before this rule is needed.
