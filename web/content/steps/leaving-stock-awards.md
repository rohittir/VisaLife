#### What it is

Each kind of stock award has its own rules when you leave.

- RSUs are taxed as wages when they vest, at the share price that day. They are not taxed when granted.
- Non-qualified stock options (NSOs) are taxed when you exercise them. The gain between your price and the share price is wages.
- Incentive stock options (ISOs) keep their special tax treatment only if you exercise within 3 months after your job ends.
- ESPP shares get the better tax treatment only if you hold them at least 2 years from the grant date and 1 year from the purchase date.

#### When

Read your award agreements before your last day.
They say what happens to unvested awards and how long you have to exercise vested options. Your plan decides those points, not tax law.

#### Forms and fees

- RSU, NSO and ESPP income that counts as wages appears on your Form W-2.
- Form 3922 reports ESPP and ISO transfers.
- If you sell ESPP shares sooner than the holding periods allow, the discount is ordinary income.

#### What to watch for

- If an award vests after you leave, the US taxes only the part linked to your work in the US. An IRS training webinar says to count the days you worked in the US between the grant date and the vest date, divided by all the days in that period. It is not formal guidance, and the tax regulation itself covers only options.
- Employers sometimes report the whole vest on your W-2. You may need a corrected W-2, or a statement with your Form 1040-NR.
- Exercising options after you leave the US adds tax questions of its own.
- Talk to a CPA before you exercise options or sell shares in the year you leave.
