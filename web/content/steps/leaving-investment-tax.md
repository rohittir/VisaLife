#### What it is

Once you are a non-resident alien, the US taxes your US investment income at a flat rate, with no deductions.
Treaty rates can lower that rate. The India treaty has its own rates.

#### When

Each time you are paid. The broker or payer withholds the tax.

#### Forms and fees

- Dividends: 30% by law. Under the India treaty the rate is 25% for an individual investor. You need a valid Form W-8BEN to get it.
- Dividends from mutual funds and ETFs set up as regulated investment companies also get the 25% rate. The 15% rate is only for companies that own at least 10% of the voting stock.
- Interest: 15% under the India treaty. Interest on bank deposits is not taxed by the US for a non-resident. Portfolio interest is also exempt, with proof of non-US status.
- Mutual fund "interest-related dividends" are exempt. Some short-term capital gain dividends are exempt, but not if you were in the US 183 days or more in the tax year.
- Gains on selling stock: a non-resident with a tax home abroad generally has foreign-source gains, which the US does not tax. If your tax home is in the US, the gain is taxed at 30% only when you were in the US 183 days or more in the tax year. The India treaty adds no separate rate.
- The broker reports your income on Form 1042-S.

#### What to watch for

- The 183 days are counted over the tax year. How the count works in the year you leave is a question for a CPA.
- Without a valid Form W-8BEN, the broker may withhold the full 30%.
- Talk to a CPA if you hold funds, REITs or foreign-listed shares. Each follows its own rule.
